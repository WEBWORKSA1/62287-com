How to Build an Event Budget That Actually Holds

By the 62287.com editorial team · Updated 2026-11-26

Most event budgets fail the same way: someone picks a venue first, falls in love with it, and every other decision gets squeezed. Flip the order and your budget holds.

1. Set the total before you shop

Decide what you can spend without debt or regret. Write it down. Every quote from now on is measured against that number, not against what other people spent.

2. Fix the guest count range

Guest count is the biggest cost lever. Food, drink, rentals, space and staff all scale per head. A list of 120 vs 90 guests can move a budget by a quarter.

3. Split by category

A common wedding split puts roughly 28% toward the venue and rentals, 30% toward catering and bar, about 11% to photo and video, and the remainder across music, flowers, attire, stationery, coordination and a contingency. Corporate events weight venue and AV more heavily. Our budget planner shows the split for each event type.

4. Calculate per-guest cost

Divide the total by guests. If the result can't cover food and drink in your city, change the format — a brunch, a cocktail reception or a weekday date — before you start cutting quality everywhere.

5. Get itemised quotes

Ask every vendor for line items: service charges, gratuities, overtime rates, travel fees, cake-cutting and corkage. Hidden fees routinely add 20–25% to food and beverage totals.

6. Keep a contingency

Hold 5–10% back. Weather plans, extra guests and overtime will claim it.

7. Track as you book

Every signed contract goes into a simple tracker: category, vendor, total, deposit paid, balance due and due date. Review it weekly.
