Relocation

Moving from St. Louis to a Small Illinois Town: A Numbers-First Checklist

By the 62278.com editorial team · Updated 2026-11-26

Every year, St. Louis-area households look across the river for more space and a lower price per square foot. Towns like Red Bud (ZIP 62278), about 36 miles southeast of the city, are a common destination. The move can be a great deal — if you run the numbers first.

1. Price the commute honestly

Downtown St. Louis is roughly 40 road miles from Red Bud. At 27 MPG and $3.10 a gallon, a five-day round-trip commute burns about $2,200 of fuel a year — and closer to $13,000 a year once wear, insurance and depreciation are counted at an all-in rate of about $0.70 per mile. Hybrid schedules change the maths dramatically: at two office days a week, that cost falls by 60%.

2. Compare property tax, not just price

Illinois property taxes are among the highest in the country as a share of home value. A cheaper house can still carry a tax bill similar to a pricier Missouri home. Ask for the last two tax bills on any listing and plug them into the mortgage calculator.

3. Check income tax and paycheck effects

Illinois has a flat state income tax; Missouri's is graduated, and the City of St. Louis adds an earnings tax for residents and people who work in the city. If you keep a city job, you may still owe the city earnings tax. Confirm with a tax professional before you move.

4. Housing: move fast, inspect carefully

Redfin's 62278 data showed a median sale price around $200K in late 2025, with homes selling in about six to seven weeks. Older homes near downtown can need roofs, electrical and drainage work — budget an inspection plus a 10% repair cushion.

5. Healthcare and schools

Red Bud has a regional hospital for everyday and emergency care; major specialists remain in the St. Louis area. Visit the local schools in person and ask about class sizes, transportation and activities.

6. Lifestyle: try before you buy

Spend a weekday evening and a festival weekend in town. Small-town life is quieter, more community-centred and less anonymous — which some people love and others don't.

Bottom line

The move works best for remote or hybrid workers, families who want space, and buyers priced out of inner-ring suburbs. If you need a daily downtown commute and big-city nightlife, factor those costs in before you sign.

Information is general and for planning only; it is not legal, tax or financial advice.
