Planning a Corporate Event: From Objective to Run-of-Show

By the 62287.com editorial team · Updated 2026-11-26

A corporate event is a business investment. Plan it like one: start with the outcome, not the venue.

Define the objective

Is it alignment, celebration, sales, recruiting or learning? Write one sentence and one measurable result — for example, “launch the new product to 200 partners and book 40 demos.”

Budget per attendee

Venue and AV typically take the largest share, followed by catering, speakers or entertainment, travel, marketing and staffing. Build per-attendee costs so leadership can compare formats.

Pick the format

  • Offsite: 15–60 people, workshops, a destination with meeting rooms.
  • Conference: 100+ people, plenary plus breakouts, registration and badges.
  • Holiday party: celebration first — great food, music and transport home.

Agenda that holds attention

Keep sessions under 45 minutes, schedule interactive blocks after lunch, and put the most important content early in the day.

AV is where events break

Confirm projector or LED resolution, microphones, recording, Wi-Fi capacity and a technician on site. Rehearse every presentation on the actual equipment.

Run-of-show

A minute-by-minute document shared with every vendor and speaker: load-in, doors, sessions, meals, transitions and load-out, each with an owner.

Measure and follow up

Send a two-question survey within 24 hours, share recordings, and report the result against the objective you set.
