How to Choose a Small-Town Industrial Site: 12 Questions to Ask First

By the 62278.com editorial team · Updated 2026-11-26

Small-town business parks can offer cheaper land, faster approvals and incentives that metro sites can't match. They also carry risks — thin labour pools, limited utilities and slower services. Use these twelve questions before you commit.

Utilities & infrastructure

  1. Power: What capacity is available at the lot line, and who provides it? Municipal utilities can sometimes move faster than investor-owned ones.
  2. Water & sewer: Main sizes and treatment capacity matter for process water. A 10-inch water main and 8-inch sanitary sewer suit most light industry.
  3. Gas: Pressure and line size for heating and processes.
  4. Broadband: Is fibre at the lot, or only nearby?

Logistics

  1. Highway access: How many turns from your dock to a state or interstate route?
  2. River, rail and air: Is barge or rail access close enough to matter for your freight? A port dock five minutes away can cut bulk costs.
  3. Trade zones: Is the site inside a Foreign Trade Zone? FTZ status can defer or reduce duties on imported inputs.

People

  1. Labour shed: How many workers live within a 30-minute drive, and who else is hiring them?
  2. Training: Is there a community-college campus with programmes you can shape?

Money & risk

  1. Incentives: Enterprise zones, sales-tax rebates on building materials, revolving loan funds and state grants can stack — get them in writing.
  2. Zoning & covenants: Confirm permitted uses, setbacks, outdoor storage rules and noise limits.
  3. Flood & soil: Pull flood maps and order a geotechnical report early.

Applying it to 62278

The Red Bud Business Park is a useful test case: about 50 acres of light-industrial/commercial lots, utilities and fibre, location within FTZ 31, and a river-port dock nearby. Run the twelve questions against it and against two competing sites — the comparison is the decision. Request site information.
