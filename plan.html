How to use these numbers

  1. Start with the guest count. It drives catering, space, rentals and staffing — cutting 20 guests saves more than any single negotiation.
  2. Fix the budget, then split it. Decide your total first, then allocate. Re-run the split if one category goes over.
  3. Price per guest is your reality check. If your per-guest number can't cover food and drink in your city, change the format (brunch, cocktail, weekday) before you change vendors.
  4. Book in order of scarcity. Venue first, then the vendors who only work one event a day (photographer, band, planner).
  5. Keep 5–10% contingency. Overtime, weather plans and last-minute guests always appear.

Figures are planning estimates. Real prices vary widely by city, season, day of week and vendor. Always get itemised quotes in writing.
